\section{Empirical-reference dependence experiments}\label{app:dependence-power}
\paragraph{Gaussian-copula illustration and paired negative control.}
To complement the population divergence in
Figure~\ref{fig:gaussian_copula_rank_example}, we compare an independent uniform
reference on $(0,1)^2$ with Gaussian-copula targets having the same uniform
marginals and correlation $\rho\in\{.2,.4\}$. We use $d=2$, $K=2$,
$N=M\in\{250,500,1000,2000\}$ and one joint view. The Gaussian-copula
construction applies the standard normal cdf coordinatewise to a correlated
normal vector. Under each allocation, the empirical reference histogram and
target histogram are rebuilt using a shared size-$K$ reference batch from fixed
reference-role positions. The statistic is
\[
 T_{\rm joint}=N\sum_{j=1}^{9}
 \frac{(\widehat p_j-\widehat q_j)^2}{\widehat q_j},\qquad
 \widehat p_j=H_{X,j}/N,\quad
 \widehat q_j=(H_{Y,j}+.1)/(M+.9).
\]
The negative control uses only coordinate 0 and three cells, with denominator
$M+.3$ in its regularized reference law. The two procedures share data, jitter,
reference positions and every permutation. It is a \emph{fixed-coordinate}
control, not an aggregate of evidence from all marginal coordinates.
The unchanged marginal law motivates the control; the finite-resolution
joint law can reflect the tested dependence differences.

\paragraph{Clayton and Gumbel alternatives.}
A separate comparison fixes $d\in\{4,20\}$, $N=M=1000$ and
$\mathsf{Beta}(2,5)$ marginals. The reference is an equicorrelated Gaussian
copula with $\rho=.6$; targets are Clayton or Gumbel copulas matched in
Kendall tau, $\tau=(2/\pi)\arcsin(.6)$, not necessarily Pearson correlation.
Clayton parameter is $\theta_C=2\tau/(1-\tau)$ and Gumbel parameter is
$\theta_G=1/(1-\tau)$. Gumbel sampling uses the positive-stable frailty
construction with denominator exponent $1/\alpha$, $\alpha=1/\theta_G$.
Both-sample Gaussian, Clayton and Gumbel nulls are included in each dimension.
Numerical sampler checks compare uniform marginals against the predeclared
DKW threshold and verify Kendall tau; all pass before the main evaluation.

We compare pair-rank $G$, random-location ME, unsmoothed Fourier features and
exact unbiased median-grid Gaussian MMD. Rank settings are $K=4$,
$\gamma=.1$, requested $L=64$ deduplicated pairs (6 actual at $d=4$, 64 at
$d=20$). All $M$ reference-role observations contribute to each histogram;
views and reference positions are fixed across the complete statistic's
allocations. Sum aggregation, shared reference batches and jitter SD $10^{-12}$
are used as in the Gaussian experiment.

\paragraph{Finite-feature and exact-kernel controls.}
For pooled observations $z$, ME uses $J=10$ locations $t_j$ sampled without
labels and $\phi_j(z)=\exp(-\|z-t_j\|^2/(2\eta^2))$.
Unsmoothed Fourier uses $J=10$ fixed draws
$\omega_j\sim\mathcal N(0,\eta^{-2}I_d)$ and features
$(\cos(\omega_j^\top z),\sin(\omega_j^\top z))_{j=1}^{J}$.
This is not Gaussian-window SCF. The scale $\eta$ is the pooled median
positive distance from at most 1000 observations; features and scale are
fixed across permutations. With $\bar\phi_X,\bar\phi_Y$ denoting role means,
$A_X,A_Y$ their sums of centered outer products, and feature dimension $h$,
\[
 \widehat V=\frac{A_X+A_Y}{N+M-1},\qquad
 T_{\rm feat}=\frac{NM}{N+M}(\bar\phi_X-\bar\phi_Y)^\top
 (\widehat V+10^{-6}I_h)^{-1}(\bar\phi_X-\bar\phi_Y).
\]
Covariance is recomputed for every allocation. These fixed random features
are not optimized locations or frequencies. The ME64/Fourier64 controls in
Appendix~\ref{app:axis-mixture} use the same feature statistic with $J=64$.
Exact MMD uses the equal mixture of five Gaussian bandwidths
$\eta 2^j$, $j=-2,\ldots,2$, fixed from the pooled median, and the unbiased
within-role U-statistic (diagonal terms excluded) minus twice the cross-role
kernel average. It is not a single-bandwidth or random-feature replacement.
All four controls share the data and label-allocation permutations.

\paragraph{Calibration, repetition and uncertainty.}
All tests use $\alpha=.05$, $B=100$ independent uniform permutations,
$p=(1+\sum_b\1\{T_b\ge T_{\rm obs}\})/101$, complete budgets and ties included;
there is no analytic chi-square critical value, mid-$p$, antithetic pairing or
stopping. Seed base is 2026100723. There are $R=4000$ blocks per null and
$R=500$ per alternative, 46000 paired blocks and 144000 method results.
There are no failed or omitted calls. This empirical-reference procedure is
not the independent-fresh-batch oracle of the population illustration.

The predeclared 32-screen family resolves a null only if its one-sided exact
binomial upper bound at confidence $1-.05/32$ is at most $.065$ and the
Bonferroni32 over-rejection test against $.05$ is not significant. All eight
Gaussian illustration screens resolve; 25 of 32 resolve overall. Seven copula
screens remain inconclusive, none significantly over-rejecting (minimum
adjusted $p=.670741$). The $d=4$ Gaussian-null rank upper bound $.06512$
is not rounded into passing. Inconclusive is not proof of intrinsic invalidity,
but the frozen rule withholds all copula power-ranking claims.

The original 20 contrasts consist of 12 pair-rank versus ME/Fourier/MMD
comparisons and eight joint versus coordinate-0 Pearson comparisons.
All remain in one family of exact two-sided McNemar tests with Holm20.
Let $n_{10},n_{01}$ be A-only and B-only rejections;
$\widehat\Delta=(n_{10}-n_{01})/R$, with descriptive pointwise normal intervals
using $\widehat{\rm SE}^2=((n_{10}+n_{01})/R-\widehat\Delta^2)/R$.
The primary inference uses the exact paired tests, not those intervals.
Qualification additionally requires resolved matching null screens for both
methods; each copula contrast requires all three common-law nulls at its
dimension. No hypothesis slot is removed after seeing its size result.

\paragraph{Findings, including non-significant and negative comparisons.}
Six Gaussian contrasts are size-qualified and Holm-significant: all four
$\rho=.4$ sizes and $\rho=.2$ at $N=M=1000,2000$. The two remaining
$\rho=.2$ comparisons are non-significant (Holm $p=1$ at 250 and $.228$ at
500), not evidence of zero difference. At $N=M=2000$, joint frequencies
are $.736$ and $.998$ versus coordinate-0 $.036$ and $.052$;
pointwise exact 95\% intervals for joint power are $[.69504,.77414]$ and
$[.98891,.99995]$. This demonstrates empirical-reference detection of these
specific dependence-only alternatives, not universal fixed-$K$ identification,
consistency, dimension-free sample complexity or failure of multivariate
geometric tests.

The Clayton/Gumbel results are descriptive rejection frequencies only.
At $d=4$, Gumbel Fourier rejection is $.970$ versus rank $.576$;
at $d=20$, Gumbel MMD is $.988$ versus rank $.952$.
All 12 copula contrasts fail the required size qualification, even where
Holm-adjusted frequency-difference tests are significant. We retain every
comparison and screen without relaxing the margin or increasing repetitions
in response to the result. Tables give full-precision decision flags beside
rounded displayed bounds.

\paragraph{Hardware, timing and audit.}
Copula blocks use AMD EPYC 7302 CPUs; Gaussian illustration blocks use Intel
Xeon Gold 6148. Every paired block shares one CPU, one numerical thread,
double precision, Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.5.2 and SciPy 1.18.1;
exact Python/compiler and kernel records are retained, with secondary
platform-stratified rates. Source/raw tails and original-worker input replay
pass, as does an independent full-coverage/tail/interval/McNemar/Holm recount.
Allocated main CPU cost is $5.6247$ core-hours; qualification adds $.0203$,
with no GPUs. Copula timing is method-component time plus charged common
allocation construction, not independently measured standalone latency or
an equal-time comparison. The paired coordinate-only timing reuses the joint
construction and is not a separate standalone runtime benchmark.
Complete statistics, configuration, CSVs and checksums accompany this source
under \texttt{empirical\_data/dependence\_20261007/}.
