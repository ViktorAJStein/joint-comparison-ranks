\paragraph{Protocol and statistic.}
We compare Pearson, $G$ (centered KL), Freeman--Tukey (FT), and
Cressie--Read $\lambda=2$ (CR$_2$), without selecting $f$ on the evaluation
outcomes. For $d\in\{20,50\}$, each class supplies 200 independent training
observations and $N=M=500$ evaluation observations. The pooled training mean
centers both evaluation samples; training labels are not used for choosing $f$.
We hold $K=4$, $L=64$ distinct random coordinate pairs ($d_{\rm sub}=2$),
reference pseudocount $\gamma=.1$, Gaussian jitter SD $10^{-12}$, and
$\alpha=.05$ fixed. Every data block shares its data, coordinate pairs,
reference-position draws, jitter and permutations across all four generators.
A shared reference batch per view is reconstructed from fixed reference-role
positions for every allocation; all $M$ observations assigned the reference
role contribute to its histogram. In each view $\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}$
with $m=(K+1)^2=25$ cells, indexed by $j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, write
\[
 \widehat p_{\ell j}=\frac{H_{X,\ell j}}{N},\qquad
 \widehat q_{\ell j}=\frac{H_{Y,\ell j}+\gamma}{M+\gamma m},\qquad
 T_f=c_fN\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{j=1}^{m}
 \widehat q_{\ell j}f(\widehat p_{\ell j}/\widehat q_{\ell j}).
\]
Here $H_{X,\ell j},H_{Y,\ell j}$ are the target/reference cell counts for the
current allocation, and $c_f=2/f''(1)$. The generators are
$(t-1)^2$, $t\log t-t+1$, $2(\sqrt t-1)^2$, and $(t^3-3t+2)/6$,
with $c_f=1,2,2,2$, respectively and continuous endpoint conventions.
All four have finite $f(0)$; FT also has finite recession slope 2. Since
$\gamma>0$, this experiment does not exercise a zero-reference-mass recession
term. Calibration uses $B=199$ independent uniform permutations, the complete
fixed budget and the plus-one upper tail, including ties. Batched count
arithmetic is an implementation optimization of these same statistics;
840 scalar/batched equivalence checks precede decision-scale evaluation.

\paragraph{Data and scope.}
Nulls are standard Gaussian, independently rotated standard Gaussian,
integer-rounded standard Gaussian, and Gaussian AR1 with covariance
$\Sigma_{ij}=.6^{|i-j|}$, $i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$.
The AR1 alternative shifts the target mean by $.4d^{-1/2}\mathbf1$.
The sparse-scale target has covariance $\operatorname{diag}(1.5^2,1,\ldots,1)$
against a standard Gaussian reference, in raw and independently rotated
coordinates. A common orthogonal matrix from a Gaussian QR decomposition
rotates both classes, including their training observations, within a paired
base block. The matched-moment mixture has equiprobable component means
$\pm\mu$, $\mu=(.8/\sqrt2,.8/\sqrt2,0,\ldots,0)$, and within-component
covariance $I_d-\mu\mu^\top$ against a standard Gaussian reference; the
implementation adds $10^{-10}I_d$ to this covariance for numerical sampling.
This is not the axis-aligned multi-component mixture diagnostic elsewhere.

There are $R=4000$ blocks per null representation and $R=500$ per alternative
representation. Seeds are independent of the comparator and representation cohorts. All 27000 base blocks, 36000 paired representation groups and 144000
generator results completed, with no failures or warnings; qualification data
are excluded. This pair-view/$B=199$ study is distinct from the
singleton-view/$B=100$ PCA audit. Its empirical results do not extend the
independent-fresh-batch oracle consistency theorem.

\paragraph{Calibration and paired inference.}
The predeclared 32 null screens use one-sided exact binomial over-rejection
tests with Bonferroni correction at family level $.05$. A screen is resolved
only if it has no significant adjusted over-rejection and its simultaneous
one-sided exact upper size bound is at most $.065$ (bound confidence
$1-.05/32$ per screen). Frequencies range from $.04425$ to $.05775$;
23 screens resolve and 9 are inconclusive, with no significant adjusted
over-rejection (Table~\ref{tab:matched-f-null}); Figure~\ref{fig:matched-f-null}
shows the distinct pointwise intervals. Inconclusive does not mean
proven miscalibration. Pointwise exact 95\% intervals are not simultaneous.

All six generator pairs in each of eight alternative/dimension/representation
strata form one family of 48 exact two-sided paired McNemar tests, with Holm
correction at $.05$. Let $n_{10},n_{01}$ count blocks rejected only by generator
A or B. The paired frequency difference is
$\widehat\Delta=(n_{10}-n_{01})/R$; reported pointwise normal95\% intervals use
$\widehat{\rm SE}^2=(n_{10}+n_{01}-(n_{10}-n_{01})^2/R)/(R(R-1))$.
These intervals are descriptive, not simultaneous substitutes for Holm.
Of 26 significant frequency differences, 12 additionally have both matching
null screens resolved; the other 14 are not promoted to controlled-size
superiority. Tables~\ref{tab:matched-f-contrasts-locshift},
\ref{tab:matched-f-contrasts-sparse-scale} and
\ref{tab:matched-f-contrasts-gmm-matched} give all 48 contrasts.
AR1 shifts use the AR1 null; raw/rotated scale use the corresponding
Gaussian null, and the mixture uses the raw Gaussian null. All planned strata,
not just favorable comparisons, remain visible.

\paragraph{Findings.}
Table~\ref{tab:matched-f-power} and Figure~\ref{fig:matched-f-power} show all
alternative frequencies. On rotated scale at $d=20$, G exceeds FT by $.100$
(pointwise paired 95\% CI $[.06935,.13065]$, Holm $p=5.03\times10^{-9}$).
On raw scale at $d=50$, G exceeds Pearson by $.186$
(CI $[.14416,.22784]$, Holm $p=1.03\times10^{-15}$).
Both matching null screens resolve for each highlighted comparison.
FT also exceeds CR$_2$ on the $d=20$ AR1 shift by $.448$, Holm
$p=9.17\times10^{-53}$, with resolved matching screens.
G and FT shift differences are not Holm-significant, no adjusted difference
is significant for rotated scale at $d=50$, and all four choices remain near
nominal rejection on the tested matched-moment mixture.
Generator choice therefore affects finite-sample detection under this fixed
procedure, not a universal ordering or a validated adaptive-$f$ selector.
Rare-reference-cell noise amplification is a possible explanation, not a
contribution-level mechanism verified by this experiment.

\paragraph{Runtime and reproducibility.}
The fixed first 32 blocks per stratum are timed in randomized generator order,
running each complete single-generator test routine on already-prepared
evaluation arrays. This includes jitter/ranks, all permutations, count hashing
inside the routine, and a common scalar-G initialization/check overhead for
EVERY generator; it excludes data generation, training-only centering and
pairwise-distance preprocessing, external input hashing, archival I/O and
replay. Shared multi-generator costs are not standalone latency.
Tables~\ref{tab:matched-f-runtime-null}--\ref{tab:matched-f-runtime-alternative}
report all means, medians and interquartile ranges; these are descriptive,
not confidence-adjusted runtime rankings. Figure~\ref{fig:matched-f-runtime}
shows every alternative/runtime point, without treating a noisy empirical
maximum as a proven winner. Descriptive runtime-budget selections require
an explicit eligible set, measured test-routine budget, calibration screens
and selection rule, not a new test chosen on evaluation labels.

All runs use one AMD EPYC 7302 CPU, Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1
and scikit-learn 1.9.0, with one numerical thread and no GPU. The prospective
cohort permits Linux 6.12.110/111 with the same exact Python build/packages;
full platform metadata and secondary kernel-stratified summaries are retained
without replacing primary denominators. All standalone timing observations
come from the 6.12.111 stratum. These batched-backend, prepared-input timings
must not be combined with external-comparator backend/hardware/pipeline
timings into a speedup or dominance claim. Main parent allocations cost
3.602 CPU-core-hours; retaining the full preparation and central-analysis
reserves gives a conservative inclusive bound 4.352 hours.
